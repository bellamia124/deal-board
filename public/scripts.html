The 60-second call

OPEN
“Hi — is this [First]? … [First], you don’t know me, and this is a cold call. Give me twenty seconds to tell you why I called, and then you can tell me to get lost.”
Radical honesty is a pattern-interrupt; offering “get lost” hands control back — people stay when they know they can leave.
FRAME
“I’m [Your Name] with Transworld. I represent one NASDAQ-listed strategic buyer — not a private-equity fund — actively acquiring survey and civil firms. I’ve closed nine of these, two here in Florida. I’m not calling to list your company. I’m calling because they’re looking in your market.”
Authority through true specifics + scarcity; the take-away (“not here to list you”) removes what they’re defending against.
DISARM
“And look — I know exactly how this sounds. Probably like every other broker who’s ever cold-called you.”
Labeling — say their objection before they do and it loses its charge.
HOOK
“Can I ask you something, [First]? How much real thought have you given to what happens to the firm when you’re ready to slow down?”
A calibrated question — makes them talk and surfaces the succession gap in their own words. Let the silence sit.
RAISE STAKES
“Here’s what I see with owners at your level: you build something for thirty years, and the exit only happens once — usually reactively, when health or burnout forces the timing. That’s when the price is worst. A strong buyer showing up while you’re still running hard is the rare version.”
Loss aversion + status; reframes doing nothing as the risk. True, so it survives scrutiny.
CLOSE
“I’m not asking you to decide anything today. Fifteen minutes, confidential, just us — and you’ll walk away knowing more about what your firm is worth to a strategic buyer than you do right now. You decide if it goes further. Worst case, you’re out fifteen minutes. What does Thursday look like?”
Micro-commitment + risk reversal; assumptive close on the calendar — ask WHEN, never IF.

The text touch — manual, 1:1, after a call

TCPA: texting a cell is $500–$1,500 per text. Send by hand, one at a time, from your phone — never a blast, never a DNC number. The board’s Copy text button (cards with a mobile number) fills it for you.
A · direct
[First] — [Your Name], Transworld. I represent one NASDAQ-listed strategic buyer (not PE) quietly acquiring survey/civil firms, and they’re active in your market. Not asking you to list — 15 confidential minutes so you’d know what [Firm] is worth to them. Worth a quick call this week? Reply STOP and you’ll never hear from me again.

Hi [First] — [Your Name], Transworld M&A. Blunt question: ever thought about what happens to [Firm] when you’re ready to step back? I represent a strategic buyer active in your space — might be worth a confidential 15 min. If not, tell me and I’m gone.
C · short
[First], [Your Name] at Transworld. A NASDAQ-listed buyer I represent is quietly acquiring firms like yours nearby. Not a listing pitch — a confidential 15-min so you know your number before timing forces it. Open to it?

Gatekeeper & the three objections

GATEKEEPER
“Hi — [Your Name] calling for [Owner]. It’s a confidential business matter about the firm, not a vendor pitch. Is he in?”
Pattern-interrupt for the front desk — “confidential business matter” is true and sits outside what they’re trained to block.
They say: “I’m not interested / not for sale.”
“Totally fair — and honestly that’s who this buyer wants, the owners who aren’t shopping themselves around. I’m not asking you to sell; I’m asking you to know your number before something forces the timing. Fifteen minutes.”
Agree, don’t fight — then reframe “not selling” into the ideal profile, and re-anchor on loss aversion.
They say: “Who’s the buyer?”
“I can’t name them on a cold call — they’re publicly traded, so I’m careful until we’re one-on-one under an NDA. What I can tell you: NASDAQ-listed, a strategic operator in your exact space, not PE. The name is the first thing you get the moment we’re confidential.”
The compliance restraint reads as credibility, not evasion — plus a curiosity gap only an NDA closes.
They say: “Just email me something.”
“I could, and it’ll sit with the other forty. Give me the fifteen minutes; if it’s not worth your time I’ll never call again and I’ll send whatever you want. Deal?”
Take-away + reciprocity + a tiny “yes” that builds momentum toward the real one.

On every call

  • Never name the buyer — describe the profile; the name comes 1:1, under NDA.
  • Never quote the revenue estimate — it's a model number, not a fact.
  • A person presses dial every time — no predictive/parallel dialing; a 🚫 DNC-listed card has no dial button — don't dial it.
  • Log the outcome on the card so the follow-up doesn't get lost.
