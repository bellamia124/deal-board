Outreach scripts

The whole script is here — call arc, text touch, gatekeeper and objection handlers. Everywhere: describe the buyer, never name it; never say the estimate out loud.

The 60-second call

OPEN
“Hi — is this [First]? … [First], you don’t know me, and this is a cold call. Give me twenty seconds to tell you why I called, and then you can tell me to get lost.”
Radical honesty is a pattern-interrupt; offering “get lost” hands control back — people stay when they know they can leave.
FRAME
“I’m Jason Curcio with Transworld. I represent one NASDAQ-listed strategic buyer — not a private-equity fund — actively acquiring survey and civil firms. I’ve closed nine of these, two here in Florida. I’m not calling to list your company. I’m calling because they’re looking in your market.”
Authority through true specifics + scarcity; the take-away (“not here to list you”) removes what they’re defending against.
DISARM
“And look — I know exactly how this sounds. Probably like every other broker who’s ever cold-called you.”
Labeling — say their objection before they do and it loses its charge.
HOOK
“Can I ask you something? How much real thought have you given to what happens to the firm when you’re ready to slow down?”
A calibrated question — makes them talk and surfaces the succession gap in their own words. Let the silence sit.
RAISE STAKES
“Here’s what I see with owners at your level: you build something for thirty years, and the exit only happens once — usually reactively, when health or burnout forces the timing. That’s when the price is worst. A strong buyer showing up while you’re still running hard is the rare version.”
Loss aversion + status; reframes doing nothing as the risk. True, so it survives scrutiny.
CLOSE
“I’m not asking you to decide anything today. Fifteen minutes, confidential, just us — and you’ll walk away knowing more about what your firm is worth to a strategic buyer than you do right now. You decide if it goes further. Worst case, you’re out fifteen minutes. What does Thursday look like?”
Micro-commitment + risk reversal; assumptive close on the calendar — ask WHEN, never IF.

Gatekeeper & the three objections

GATEKEEPER
“Hi — Jason Curcio calling for [Owner]. It’s a confidential business matter about the firm, not a vendor pitch. Is he in?”
Pattern-interrupt for the front desk — “confidential business matter” is true and sits outside what they’re trained to block.
They say: “I’m not interested / not for sale.”
“Totally fair — and honestly that’s who this buyer wants, the owners who aren’t shopping themselves around. I’m not asking you to sell; I’m asking you to know your number before something forces the timing. Fifteen minutes.”
Agree, don’t fight — then reframe “not selling” into the ideal profile, and re-anchor on loss aversion.
They say: “Who’s the buyer?”
“I can’t name them on a cold call — they’re publicly traded, so I’m careful until we’re one-on-one under an NDA. What I can tell you: NASDAQ-listed, a strategic operator in your exact space, not PE. The name is the first thing you get the moment we’re confidential.”
The compliance restraint reads as credibility, not evasion — plus a curiosity gap only an NDA closes.
They say: “Just email me something.”
“I could, and it’ll sit with the other forty. Give me the fifteen minutes; if it’s not worth your time I’ll never call again and I’ll send whatever you want. Deal?”
Take-away + reciprocity + a tiny “yes” that builds momentum toward the real one.

Subject: Who handles ownership questions at [Firm]?
[First] — you’re probably not the right person for this, and that’s fine. I represent a buyer active with survey firms in [state]. I just need to reach whoever handles ownership decisions at [Firm]. Point me their way and I’ll leave you out of it entirely. [signature]
What it means on a dial: a P1 you’re calling has likely had touches 1–2 already. You’re not introducing the idea — you’re the human behind the emails. Open with that: “I’m the person behind the notes you may have seen.”
RE-ENGAGEMENT · FOR A PROSPECT GONE QUIET (not part of the cold 5 — send after a flicker of interest)
The value-tool nudge NO-CALL CTA
Subject: A number for [Firm] — no call required
[First] — I’ll make this easy. If a call feels like too much before you know whether it’s even worth it, here’s the shortcut: a quick tool that gives you a rough value range for [Firm] in about 30 seconds. No obligation, nothing shared, and no one calls unless you ask. → the “✉ Re-engage” chip on the card pastes this with a per-firm link built in (their submission lights up the card) If the range is interesting, we talk. If it’s not, you’re out 30 seconds. Either way you’ll know where you stand. [signature]
Why it’s separate: a link in a cold first touch hurts deliverability during warmup. This one is for someone who opened, half-replied, or you spoke to once — the low-commitment on-ramp to the readiness intake. The range it gives is a rule-of-thumb, not a valuation.
Two links, two jobs: the range-finder (…/public/value/) is this soft nudge — good on LinkedIn and in your signature too. The readiness intake (…/public/valuation/) is the move-forward gate you send after a yes — completing it is the real seriousness test, and it drops a 📋 INTAKE flag on the card.

Rules that don't bend

  • Never name the buyer in outreach — describe the profile; the name is disclosed 1:1, under NDA.
  • Never quote the revenue estimate on a call — it's a model number, not a fact.
  • Assign before you dial — one caller per firm, always.
  • Hand-email = log "Emailed" — that's what turns their automation off.
  • ALREADY ACQUIRED = competitor, not a lead — skip it.
  • Log every reply same day so the whole team sees it.
